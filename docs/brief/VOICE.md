# How Foodbiz should sound

**Warm, considered, modern.** Like a thoughtful host talking to you across a table, not a brand selling to you.
Confident without showing off. Indian without dressing it up.

## Three rules
1. **Write what you would actually say.** If a sentence sounds like a brochure when read out loud, rewrite it.
2. **Specific beats decorative.** "A six-course menu built around your family's regional dishes" beats "a curated
   culinary journey". Details do the work adjectives cannot.
3. **Heritage comes through the food, not the words.** Name the dish, the region, the technique. Never "ancient
   wisdom" or "the soul of India".

## Do
- Use the founders' words: "hassle-free", "leave it to us", "be present", "food and experience".
- "We" and "you". Short sentences, plain words. Concrete nouns: bhakri, dal, copper plates, a live dosa counter.
- A little dry wit, once a page at most.
- Numbers only if the client has confirmed them. **None are confirmed yet**, so no numbers for now.

## Never use
- **Catering cliches:** bespoke, curated, crafted, exquisite, gourmet (as a boast), elevated, hand-picked,
  artisanal, culinary journey, culinary excellence, premium, world of flavour, passionate about food, every detail.
- **Over-the-top luxury:** reverence, sacred, soulful, ancient wisdom, art of offering, intentional hospitality.
- **Corporate jargon:** solutions, leverage, world-class, best-in-class, turnkey, end-to-end, holistic, seamless,
  touchpoint, ecosystem.
- **Wedding cliches:** your special day, the big day, fairytale, dream wedding, magical moments, unforgettable
  memories, once-in-a-lifetime, happily ever after.
- Opening with "From intimate dinners to grand celebrations..." or any "from x to y" flourish.

## Headline shapes that work
1. One short, plain sentence. "We cook. We host. You enjoy your own event."
2. A polite reversal. "We're not in the business of food. We're in the business of food and experience."
3. A question that is not a sales question. "Whose recipes are at your table?"
4. Three concrete nouns. "Bhakri, biryani, beef wellington. The brief is yours; the food is ours."

## Before and after
**Before:** "We believe every gathering is sacred. With every plate, we present a story of grace and intentionality."
**After:** "Every event has a job to do: bring people together, mark a milestone, make a brand land. Our job is the
food and the hosting around it."

**When in doubt:** read it as if Amar were saying it on a call. If he would say it differently, rewrite it.
